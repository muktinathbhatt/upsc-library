Book 01 · Orientation

Syllabus &
Master Roadmap

UPSC does not test Economics as isolated definitions. It tests whether you can connect incentives, institutions, data and human outcomes—and then choose or defend a policy under constraints.

Outcome: By the end of this roadmap, every Economics topic will have a home, a study sequence, a revision loop and an exam output.

1. How to use this library

Read Books 2–10 in order once. After each chapter, close the page and reconstruct its central causal chain on paper. For example: policy rate rises → borrowing becomes costlier → interest-sensitive demand weakens → inflationary pressure may ease, subject to transmission and supply conditions. That chain—not the name of the instrument—is usable knowledge.

Run Books 11 and 12 in parallel after the foundations. Book 11 trains recognition and elimination for Prelims. Book 12 trains selection, structure and judgment for Mains. Current figures should be layered onto these concepts from official releases rather than memorised without context.

2. Official syllabus decoded

Prelims, General Studies Paper I

Economic and Social Development—Sustainable Development, Poverty, Inclusion, Demographics, Social Sector Initiatives, etc.; plus current events and economic geography that frequently overlap with the economy.

Mains, General Studies III

Indian economy; planning and mobilisation of resources; growth, development and employment; inclusive growth; government budgeting; agriculture, subsidies, MSP, PDS, buffer stocks and food security; food processing; land reforms; liberalisation and industrial policy; infrastructure; and investment models.

The word “etc.” in Prelims and the broad phrasing in Mains mean preparation must also cover the working vocabulary behind policy: national income, inflation, monetary and fiscal policy, banking, taxation, external sector, exchange rates, institutions, welfare delivery and human development.

What UPSC expects at each stage

  • Prelims: distinguish similar concepts, follow balance-sheet or policy effects, read statements precisely and connect current events to basics.
  • Mains: define the issue, explain mechanisms, use Indian context, evaluate trade-offs, offer feasible reforms and conclude with constitutional or developmental purpose.
  • Essay and interview: see economy as a system of human choices, power, opportunity, sustainability and state capacity.

3. Topic-to-book map

Book 2 gives the language of economics and national accounting. Books 3–6 explain the macroeconomy: growth, jobs, money, inflation, public finance and the external sector. Books 7–9 cover Indian production systems and state-market coordination: agriculture, industry, infrastructure, planning and resource mobilisation. Book 10 evaluates who participates in growth and who benefits. Books 11–12 convert understanding into marks.

5. The study method

The five-layer note

  1. Meaning: define the concept in one clean sentence.
  2. Mechanism: show the causal chain or flow.
  3. Indian context: institutions, sectors and constraints.
  4. Debate: benefits, costs, winners, losers and time horizon.
  5. Exam output: one MCQ trap, one diagram and one Mains conclusion.

Use spaced revision on Day 1, Day 7 and Day 30. A revision is successful only if it requires retrieval: blank-page recall, flash questions, MCQs or a written answer. Rereading alone produces familiarity, not dependable recall.

6. Source hierarchy

  1. Authority: UPSC notification and syllabus.
  2. Core annual documents: Union Budget and Economic Survey.
  3. Monetary and financial: RBI reports, bulletins and policy statements.
  4. Data: MoSPI, PLFS, NSO, Census, NFHS and relevant ministries.
  5. Evaluation: parliamentary committees, CAG, NITI Aayog and credible research institutions.
Currency rule: Whenever a number matters, record its indicator, unit, period, source and whether it is provisional. Never mix fiscal year, calendar year and survey-period figures.
